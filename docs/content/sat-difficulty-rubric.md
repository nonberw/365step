# Editorial SAT difficulty rubric (2026-11-02)

These ratings describe the reasoning demanded by the actual wording and options. They are not measured item difficulty, calibrated SAT parameters, or a score conversion.

| Dimension | Low | Moderate | High |
|---|---|---|---|
| Independent reasoning steps | one retrieval/substitution | two connected ideas | at least three connected decisions |
| Parameters and restrictions | all quantities supplied | infer a missing value | reason about cases, domains or consistency |
| Language density | explicit local relation | integrate clauses or two short texts | reconcile qualifications and competing explanations |
| Transformations | direct definition | familiar algebra/data comparison | combine representations or nonroutine transformations |
| Distractors | unrelated or contradicted | typical local mistake | plausible conclusions differing in scope or causal assumptions |

Easy: one local rule, definition or substitution. Medium: standard transformations, contextual inference or comparison of two views; length alone does not make an item Hard. Hard: multiple dependent decisions plus a substantive restriction, or dense qualified reasoning with plausible competing interpretations. Routine agreement, explicitly signalled transitions and direct ratios are not Hard.

All 495 previously Hard IDs have an explicit editorial decision and rationale in `shared/satDifficulty.json`. Most were downgraded after examining conditions and distractors; previous labels remain in runtime `difficultyReview.previous`. These decisions do not constitute a complete independent answer audit of all 1606 legacy items. New items have their own reasoning-based rating. Statistical calibration requires representative student trials and external validation.

Exact duplicate conditions retain their IDs and option ordering for old URLs/history. `canonicalId` suppresses redundant cards in new selection; generated families are reinforcement, R&W passages shorter than 25 words are warmups. Assessment selection uses only assessment items, suppresses duplicate conditions across modules and caps a parameter family at two per test. Structural family detection recognises numeric variants with identical wording; semantic near-duplicates still require editorial review.

Legacy option order stays stable. The selector balances correct-option positions and topic deficits; it does not rewrite answers already saved as indices. The full bank therefore still has a legacy position skew. Difficulty/position/topic preferences may relax when necessary; uniqueness/family constraints do not.
